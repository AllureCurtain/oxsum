## Recently completed

- 2026-10-03 Hold sweeper (issue #13): a background job settles gateway holds older than `OXSUM_HOLD_TIMEOUT` (default 30m, validated at startup: parseable and at least 60s) at 0 with settlement kind `swept`, releasing the whole freeze and marking the anomaly in the record for the admin page. The sweeper finds stale holds in `oxsum.open_holds`, a small watch table owned by this issue — product.md's `requests` table (item 5) does not exist yet, and the ledger's pending layer says how much is held but not by which request or since when. The gateway writes the row before taking the hold and deletes it when the turn settles; the ledger stays the source of truth for amounts and settled-ness, and the sweeper and a late settlement share the derived settlement key (`oxsum_core::settlement_key_for`), so exactly one takes effect and the other sees `Conflict("hold already settled")`. Covered by `crates/server/tests/sweep.rs` (stale hold swept at 0 with kind `swept`, young hold untouched, row-without-hold cleaned up without a write, and the sweeper/late-settlement race driven 10 times) and `crates/server/tests/gateway.rs` (a settled turn leaves no watch row; a stalled turn is swept end-to-end), plus `OXSUM_HOLD_TIMEOUT` parsing tests in `crates/server/src/config.rs`. The decision is recorded in docs/decisions.md ("a small watch table for the hold sweeper").
- 2026-10-03 Settlement-hold pairing (issue #10): a settlement now names the hold it releases (`holdKey`); the server reads the hold's amount from the hold entry in the ledger, so the request carries no amount to assert. One hold settles at most once: the settlement entry's idempotency key is derived from the hold's key (`oxsum_core::settlement_key_for`), and the ledger's idempotency gate refuses a second, different settlement of the same hold with `CONFLICT` ("hold already settled"), inside the append — concurrent settlements racing one hold cannot both release it. Retrying the identical settlement replays it. Naming a hold that is not outstanding is `HoldNotFound`, answered 404; the `funded_reservations` limit stays as the backstop. The gateway, which owns both halves of a turn, settles under the hold key it took (`req-<id>:hold`). Covered by `crates/core/tests/wallet.rs` (paired settle, discharged-hold refusal, the race), the rewritten generative oracle (paired `Settle`, `HoldNotFound` prediction), and `crates/server/tests/api.rs` (404, 409).

- 2026-10-02 Channels and versioned model prices: channels and their prices are oxsum's own tables now (`oxsum.channels`, `oxsum.channel_prices`), and a price change appends a version instead of overwriting one — a trigger, not a convention, refuses an update or a delete. A gateway request resolves its channel and version once, before the freeze, carries them through the turn, and writes both into the settlement beside the prices and the token counts, so a bill from any day stays checkable against the configuration it was written under. Upstream credentials are sealed with AES-256-GCM under `OXSUM_SECRET_KEY` with only the last four characters readable, and startup opens every stored credential once, naming the missing key instead of failing a request that has already been relayed. The platform admin reaches `/api/v1/admin` with `OXSUM_ADMIN_TOKEN` (constant-time compared, at least 16 characters): create or repoint a channel, append a price version, list channels with their current versions, read one channel's whole history. A model belongs to one channel, so pricing one that another channel serves is `CONFLICT`; the environment now only seeds a database that has no channels. Covered by `crates/core/tests/channels.rs` (versions, the trigger, the conflict, sealing and tampering, the bootstrap), `crates/server/tests/admin.rs` (the surface and its refusals) and a gateway test that changes a price while a streamed turn is in flight: the turn in flight settles at the version it started on and the next one at the new version.

- 2026-10-02 The gateway loop: `POST /v1/chat/completions` and `GET /v1/models` relay to one configured upstream, freezing an upper bound from the request text and the output ceiling *before* the call and settling against upstream's usage afterwards. The freeze, the priced usage, the local estimate and the settlement record are all arithmetic in `crates/core/src/billing.rs`; the relay is an `async-stream` generator that forwards upstream's frames, reads the usage out of the last one, and awaits the settlement before it ends, so a finished stream is a settled bill. The write itself runs in a task of its own, because an OpenAI SDK client hangs up the moment it reads the terminator — which over a real socket lands while the entry is being appended, and an append cancelled there used to leave the freeze reserved with nothing left to release it. A client that does cut a turn short cancels the upstream call and settles what had been forwarded as `client_cancelled`, from upstream's counts when it had already reported them; an upstream error or an unreachable upstream charges nothing and releases the freeze whole; usage above the freeze is charged at the freeze and recorded as `capped`. Every response carries `x-oxsum-request-id`, and `req-<id>:hold` / `req-<id>:settle` are the entries it wrote. `crates/server/tests/gateway.rs` runs a scripted upstream in-process and covers all of it, and the loop was driven by the official OpenAI Python SDK against a mock provider with nothing but `base_url` changed — its streaming call is what exposed the cancelled append.
